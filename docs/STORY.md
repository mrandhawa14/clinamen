# Terrarium — Story mode: The Archivist

Status: draft v1 · 2026-11-01

> *The record of a world called Earth is damaged. You are an Archivist. You can
> re-run the universe from its first moment. Rebuild how Earth came to be, one
> accident at a time. But every run is different, and fate does not always agree.*

---

## 1. Premise

Far in the future, the **Archive** keeps the histories of worlds. Its record of
one small blue planet, Earth, is damaged: the outline survives (a star, a moon,
oceans, life, minds), but not the chain of chances that made it.

You are an **Archivist**: a mind that can run a universe from its first moment
and watch it unfold. You can't build anything. You can only **tip the dice**:
make an accident happen, choose when, or leave it to fate.

Your task: re-run the universe and rebuild Earth's history. The twist is that
every run is different. Fate rolls its own dice, timing changes outcomes, and
your Earth may turn out unlike the real one. **The story continues with the
world you get**, not the one you were aiming for. At the end, the Archive
records *your* Earth.

### Why this story

- It explains the player's power: you can trigger accidents but not control
  results.
- It teaches real science: every chapter is a real step in Earth's history.
- Failure is part of the story, not a game-over: a world without a moon is
  still a world, and the Archive is curious about it.

## 2. Shape

- **Optional.** The sandbox is open from the start. First-time players are
  invited to begin with the story.
- **8 chapters, 1–2 hours in total**, 10–15 minutes each.
- **Story worlds run fast.** Billions of years pass in minutes, with the
  director camera moving to each event. Sandbox worlds use the normal world
  clock.
- **One world all the way through.** The world you make in chapter 1 is the
  world you finish with.
- **Saved between chapters.** Players can stop after any chapter.
- **The Archivist's world joins the gallery** at the end and can be continued
  in the sandbox.

## 3. The narrator: the Archive

The Archive speaks in short lines on screen (voice-over can come later). It is
an old, calm, curious mind. It has seen many worlds and is still delighted by
each one.

**Voice rules**

1. Short. One or two sentences per line, usually under 20 words.
2. Present tense. *"The first star is forming."*
3. Curious, never commanding. It suggests and wonders; it doesn't order.
   *"You could wait. Or you could choose the moment."*
4. One fact per line, true and plain. No jargon without the meaning next to it.
5. Never blames the player. A different outcome is *interesting*, not wrong.
6. Silence is allowed. Big moments (first star, the impact, first life) get no
   words until after they happen.
7. Calls the player "Archivist" rarely, and only at important moments.

**Examples of right and wrong**

| Right | Wrong |
|---|---|
| "Hydrogen. Almost nothing else yet." | "Welcome to Terrarium! Let's learn the controls!" |
| "The water came too early. It boiled away into space. The Archive has seen this before." | "You failed! Try again." |
| "This world has no moon. Its seasons will wander. Let's see what lives here." | "Mission failed: no moon." |

## 4. Chapters

Each chapter lists: what happens, what it teaches, the Archive's key lines, and
what happens if fate (or the player) goes another way.

### Chapter 1 — First Light
*Era 1 · teaches: camera, zoom, time speed*

- Black screen. A faint haze of gas appears. The camera drifts.
- The Archive invites the player to look around, zoom in, and speed time up.
- Gravity pulls the gas into clumps. One clump grows hot and dense, and the
  first star ignites (silence until it does).

> "Before anything, there is only this. Hydrogen, a little helium."
> "Nothing here can be built. It can only gather."
> *(the star ignites)* "…There. The first light."

**No branch:** gravity always wins. Chapter 1 can't go wrong; it's for wonder.

### Chapter 2 — The Forge
*Era 2 · teaches: resources come from history*

- Stars live and die over millions of years in seconds. Massive ones explode
  as supernovae, throwing out oxygen, carbon, silicon and iron.
- A resources panel appears for the first time, filling as stars die.

> "Stars are forges. Everything heavier than helium is made inside them."
> "The oxygen in every ocean that will ever exist is being made now."
> "The Archive can't give you iron. Only stars can."

**Variation (fate):** how many massive stars die nearby decides how much iron and
oxygen the region gets. Little iron here means a weaker core later (chapter 3
mentions it).

### Chapter 3 — A World Is Born
*Era 3 · teaches: accidents, fate vs trigger, timing*

- Dust and gas around a young sun form a disc; a molten planet forms in it.
- A Mars-sized body is on a crossing orbit. The first accident card appears:
  **Moon-forming impact**, with its window shown as a bar that closes.
- The player can trigger it now, later in the window, or let fate decide.

> "Earth's record says it had a large moon. Something hit it, very early."
> "You could wait for fate. Or you could choose the moment."
> *(after impact, silence; the ring forms; the moon gathers)*
> "A moon. It will steady this world's tilt for billions of years."

**Branches**

| What happened | Result | Archive |
|---|---|---|
| Triggered early in the window | Big moon, very short days | "A heavy moon, and days only a few hours long. They'll lengthen." |
| Right time | Earth-like moon | "Close to the record." |
| Fate rolled a smaller impact | Small moon | "A small moon. The tilt will be less steady." |
| Window closed, nothing hit | No moon | "No moon. This world's seasons will wander. Let's see what lives here." |

The story continues in every case.

### Chapter 4 — Rain
*Era 3–4 · teaches: timing changes outcomes, the 2-day undo*

- The planet cools. A crust forms. Icy bodies cross the young system.
- Accident card: **Water from space**. The window is long, and the card warns
  that the planet is still hot early on.
- This is where the **undo** is introduced: the Archive explains that a choice
  can be taken back for a while (in story mode, the undo window is shortened
  to fit the chapter; the rule is the same).

> "The record shows oceans. The water came from the sky."
> *(if triggered too early)* "Too soon. The planet was still molten, and the
> water boiled into space. You can take this back, for now."
> *(if right)* "Steam, then rain. It will rain for a very long time."

**Branches:** too early (water lost; undo offered), right time (oceans), late (a
shallow sea), or fate brings less ice (a drier world). A dry world continues to
chapter 5 with very little water, which makes life rarer.

### Chapter 5 — The Spark
*Era 5 · teaches: fate, and that failure is possible*

- Warm, shallow seas; tide pools; volcanoes. The camera lowers to the coast.
- Accident card: **Life starts**. Unlike earlier cards, the player can only
  *encourage* it: it rolls fate either way, with better odds in good
  conditions (warm, wet, chemistry available).

> "No one knows how likely this is. It happened once, as far as we know."
> *(waiting)* "Nothing yet. That's normal."
> *(life starts; silence; green mats spread through the shallows)*
> "…Something here is copying itself."

**If life never starts** before the window closes: the chapter ends with a
sterile world. The Archive offers a **branch**: return to just before the
window and let fate roll again. This teaches branching, and the limit of one
branch per world.

> "This world stayed quiet. The Archive could return to the moment before, once.
> Fate will roll again; it may not go differently."

### Chapter 6 — The Long Wait
*Era 6 · teaches: the accident tree, "impossible by nature, explained"*

- Billions of years pass. The **accident tree** opens for the first time,
  showing what's possible now, what's not yet possible (with reasons), and what
  was missed.
- Three accidents happen in this chapter, by fate or player:
  **oxygen photosynthesis → Great Oxidation**, **a cell swallows a bacterium**,
  **snowball Earth thaws**.
- The player can try something out of order and see nature's answer
  (*"Complex cells need oxygen. This world has almost none yet."*).

> "Life will now be small for a very long time. Most of history is this."
> "Oxygen is poison to most of what lives here. It's also a fuel."
> *(the swallowed bacterium)* "One cell ate another and didn't digest it. Every
> plant and animal will come from this."

**Branch:** if the swallowed-bacterium accident never happens, life stays
microbial. The story jumps to the ending with a microbial world (see
section 5).

### Chapter 7 — The Fall
*Era 6 · teaches: hard choices, permanence*

- Complex life spreads onto land. Great creatures rule (they emerged from the
  simulation, not scripted).
- An asteroid appears on a crossing course. Accident card:
  **A ruling species falls**. The player chooses: let it hit, or let it pass
  (fate still decides if the player doesn't).
- After 2 (story) days, whatever happened becomes permanent. The Archive marks
  the moment.

> "The record says something fell from the sky, and the rulers of the world
> ended."
> "Smaller creatures survived. Some of them became us."
> *(the choice)* "This one is yours, if you want it."
> *(when it becomes permanent)* "This is history now."

**Branches:** impact → a new group rises; no impact → the rulers stay and the
world continues differently (the Archive is openly curious about this one).

### Chapter 8 — Eyes Open
*Era 6 · teaches: the ending, opens the sandbox*

- Accident card: **Intelligence**. Fate's odds depend on everything before.
- If intelligence appears: lights spread on the night side. The camera follows
  one of the new minds to a high place at night. They build their first
  telescope and point it at the sky… and the view turns, slowly, toward the
  camera. Toward you. Silence.

> "They're looking up."
> *(long pause)*
> "The record is restored, Archivist. Not the Earth we lost. An Earth."
> "Every world after this is yours to make."

The sandbox opens with a final card: **"Begin a new world."**

## 5. Endings

The Archive records whatever Earth the player made. The ending adapts:

| World at the end | Ending |
|---|---|
| Moon, oceans, life, minds | **Eyes Open**: the telescope turns toward you. |
| Minds, but no moon | Same ending; the Archive notes their sky has no moon, and wonders what their myths will be. |
| Complex life, no minds | **The Garden**: a living world that never looks up. "Not every world needs to be seen to matter." |
| Microbial life only | **The Quiet Sea**: "Life, but small, forever. Most worlds may be like this." |
| No life | **The Stone**: "A world of rain and rock. The Archive keeps it too." |

Every ending:
- adds the world to the gallery with its full history,
- shows a short **"Your Earth vs the record"** comparison (which accidents
  matched, which went another way),
- opens the sandbox.

## 6. Science notes (keep it true)

| Chapter | Real basis | Simplified for play |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Early gas ~¾ hydrogen, ¼ helium; first stars formed from collapsing clouds | Millions of years in seconds |
| 2 | Elements heavier than helium are forged in stars; supernovae spread them | One region, a handful of stars |
| 3 | Giant-impact hypothesis for the Moon (leading explanation) | One impactor, clear window |
| 4 | Earth's water likely delivered partly by asteroids (debated) | Marked as a hypothesis in-game |
| 5 | Origin of life unknown; odds unknown | Odds tuned for play; the Archive says so |
| 6 | Great Oxidation ~2.4 billion years ago; mitochondria from endosymbiosis; snowball Earth episodes | Three events in one chapter |
| 7 | Chicxulub impact ~66 million years ago ended non-avian dinosaurs | "Ruling species" is whatever emerged in this world |
| 8 | Intelligence evolved once to our level, as far as we know | Fate's odds depend on the world's history |

## 7. Open questions

- **Voice-over:** text only at first; a recorded voice later?
- **Music:** a quiet score that changes per era (synth for space, warmer
  instruments for life)?
- **Replay:** after finishing, can players replay chapters with a new seed?
- **The record's details:** should the "real Earth" record be shown as a
  timeline the player fills in as they go?
